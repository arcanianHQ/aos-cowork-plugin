# The Hero's Journey

> **What it is.** An 11-beat narrative arc. The *customer* is the hero; the brand
> is the guide. Run once, it yields **11 distinct content pieces** — one series —
> that together walk a prospect from "I have an unspoken problem" to "I trust this
> brand to help me solve it."
>
> *Credit: the Hero's Journey / monomyth is Joseph Campbell, "The Hero with a
> Thousand Faces" (1949). Applied here as a content-series structure. See `NOTICE`.*

## Arc

Persuasion through identification. The audience does not see the brand as the
protagonist — they see *themselves*. Each beat advances the same prospect through a
recognisable transformation. By beat 11 the audience has lived the whole arc and the
brand has appeared, repeatedly, as the dependable guide — never the loud hero.

The arc works because it is **not a pitch repeated 11 times**. It is one story told
in 11 moves, and a prospect who follows the series is far more converted than one
who saw the same offer 11 times.

## Beats — the 11-piece series

Each beat is one content piece. The default content type is `linkedin-post`
(the canonical single-platform run); any beat may be re-typed per piece.

| # | Beat | Narrative job | Suggested type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The Ordinary World** | Show the prospect's status quo — recognisable, not yet questioned. | linkedin-post |
| 2 | **The Call to Adventure** | Name the problem / opportunity the prospect has been ignoring. | linkedin-post |
| 3 | **Refusal of the Call** | Voice the prospect's hesitation out loud — "this isn't worth it / now isn't the time." | linkedin-post |
| 4 | **Meeting the Guide** | The brand enters — as guide, not hero. Establish credibility quietly. | linkedin-post |
| 5 | **Crossing the Threshold** | The first concrete step — what changing actually looks like. | linkedin-post |
| 6 | **Tests, Allies, Enemies** | The real obstacles on the path, and what helps. Honest, not fear-mongering. | blog-post |
| 7 | **The Ordeal** | The hardest part — the moment most prospects quit. Name it; do not minimise it. | linkedin-post |
| 8 | **The Reward** | What the prospect gains on the other side. Concrete, specific, earned. | linkedin-post |
| 9 | **The Road Back** | Integrating the change — making it stick, avoiding regression. | email |
| 10 | **The Resurrection** | Proof: a real transformation. Reference / case study. The arc made true. | blog-post |
| 11 | **Return with the Elixir** | The prospect is now equipped — soft, confident invitation to begin. | linkedin-post |

## Platform note

The canonical run is **single-platform** — all 11 beats as LinkedIn posts — so the
series reads as one coherent voice in one feed. But the framework is platform-agnostic:
the table above already re-types beats 6 and 10 to `blog-post` (deeper proof beats
warrant long-form) and beat 9 to `email` (integration is a nurture moment). A series
**may span multiple content types** — that is expected, not an exception.

## When to use

- A considered purchase with real hesitation in the buying decision (beats 3 and 7
  carry the arc).
- The brand can credibly be a *guide* — has done this many times, has proof for beat 10.
- The audience is reachable repeatedly over a 4–8 week window (a series, not a one-shot).

## When NOT to use

- Impulse / low-consideration purchase — the 11-beat arc is overkill.
- No real proof story for beat 10 — the arc collapses without the Resurrection.
- A one-off announcement — use a single piece, not a series.
