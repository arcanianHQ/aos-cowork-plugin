# Structure — Story → Lesson (LinkedIn post)

> The narrative LinkedIn-post skeleton. Five sections. Fits any beat whose job is
> to **tell a moment** — a scene the reader lives, ending in one earned lesson.

## Sections

| # | Section | Job | Length hint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scene** | Drop the reader into a concrete moment — a time, a place, a person. No preamble. Doubles as the hook (must land above the fold). | 1–3 short lines |
| 2 | **Tension** | What was at stake / wrong / uncertain in that moment. The reader should feel the friction. | 2–3 short paragraphs |
| 3 | **Turn** | What changed — the decision, the realisation, the action taken. | 1–2 short paragraphs |
| 4 | **Lesson** | The single transferable point the story proves. Stated plainly, once. | 1–2 short paragraphs |
| 5 | **Bridge** | Connect the lesson to the reader's own situation — a soft pointer forward. | 1 line |

## Fit

- Beats whose narrative job is to **tell a moment** rather than make an argument.
- Hero's Journey beats 1 (Ordinary World), 7 (The Ordeal), 10 (Resurrection) when
  kept as a short-form post.
- Before-After-Bridge beats 1 (Familiar Pain), 5 (Resolved Day).
- Any beat where a real, specific scene will land harder than a stated claim.

## Anti-patterns

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| Open with "Let me tell you a story" | Open *inside* the scene |
| A vague, composite "someone" | One concrete person / moment (anonymised if needed) |
| Two lessons | One lesson — the story earns exactly one |
| A fabricated scene | Real moments only; if there is no real story, use `hook-insight-cta` instead |
| Lesson that doesn't follow from the story | The turn must logically produce the lesson |
