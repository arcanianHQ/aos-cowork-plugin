# Structure — Problem → Framework → Proof (blog post)

> The own-blog authority skeleton. Six sections. The brand earns authority in the
> middle section by giving *structure*, not just facts.

## Sections

| # | Section | Job | Length hint |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Problem framing** | Name the reader's problem in their register; validate it is real and non-trivial. | 150–250 words |
| 2 | **Why this matters now** | What's at stake, what getting it wrong costs. Honest accounting, not fear. | 100–200 words |
| 3 | **The framework** | The brand's POV on how to think about the topic — named categories / decision axes. This section earns the authority. | 400–800 words |
| 4 | **Applied examples** | Work the framework through 2–4 real cases. Cite `products.md` where relevant. Mix own + public examples. | 300–600 words |
| 5 | **Common mistakes** | Name the specific failure modes. Generic mistakes read as filler. | 150–300 words |
| 6 | **What to do next** | One practical next step. Soft CTA + internal link to a related piece. | 100–200 words |

## Fit

- The default for the deep beats of a series — the ones routed to `blog-post`.
- Hero's Journey beat 6 (Tests, Allies, Enemies) and beat 10 (Resurrection — as a
  written-up case study).
- Before-After-Bridge beats 6 (Second-Order Win) and 7 (Proof It's Real).
- Any beat whose job is to be the durable, linkable asset the feed beats point into.

## Anti-patterns

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|---|---|
| A list of 10 tips with no framework | Framework first, examples *within* it |
| "In this post I will explain…" | Open with the reader's problem |
| Close with "I hope this helped!" | Close with the next concrete decision |
| Vague claims ("quality matters") | Specific, contestable claims |
| Link only to own product pages | Mix own + external authoritative sources |
