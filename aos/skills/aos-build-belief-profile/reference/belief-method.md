# Belief method — how to surface a decision-maker's beliefs

The methodology behind `aos-build-belief-profile`. This is the Arcanian
belief-tracking approach (Avatar lineage) — not a new framework. Read it before
drafting any profile.

## The core principle

> **Every business result is a creation that reveals the beliefs that created it.**

```
BELIEFS → CREATE → RESULTS → APPEAR TO "PROVE" → REINFORCED BELIEFS
```

So we work **backwards**:

```
OBSERVABLE RESULT → ASK "what belief would create this?" → CANDIDATE BELIEF
```

A belief is rarely stated outright. It shows up two ways: in **what the person
says** (linguistic markers) and in **what their business does** (results). A
belief confirmed by *both* streams is a **primary** belief — those are the ones
that matter.

## Transparency — why beliefs hide

Beliefs sit on a spectrum:

- **Visible** — the person knows it is a belief and can argue it.
- **Semi-transparent** — they sense it but treat it as mostly-true.
- **Transparent** — they experience it as *reality*, not as a belief at all.
  *"That's just how this market works."* Transparent beliefs are the most
  growth-blocking and the hardest to surface — they never get questioned.

When profiling, label each belief's transparency. The transparent ones are the
priority for the growth-blocking shortlist.

## The five required dimensions

Every profiled person needs all five. If a dimension has no evidence, write
*"not yet evidenced"* — never guess.

| Dimension | The question it answers | Where the evidence lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity beliefs** | What kind of person do they believe they are in this role? | First-person self-description; how they talk about their own role |
| **Capability beliefs** | What do they believe they *can* and *can't* do? Where are the self-imposed ceilings? | "We can't…", "I'm not the kind of person who…", delegation behaviour |
| **Value beliefs** | What do they believe *matters* in this business? | What they protect, what they refuse to compromise, what they brag about |
| **Fears** | What scares them — about the business, the market, their role? | "What bothers me is…", "I'm worried that…", avoidance behaviour |
| **JTBD register** | In their own words, what job do they hire marketing to do? | How they describe what marketing is *for*; complaints about past marketing |

## Linguistic markers

Scan harvested transcripts and correspondence for these.

**Limiting markers** (signal a capability or worth ceiling):
`just`, `only`, `small`, `a bit of`, `trying to`, `hoping to`, `maybe`,
`we can't`, `our market won't`, `that's just how it is`, `affordable`,
`I'm not the kind of person who`, `who am I to`.

**Empowering / ownership markers** (signal a confident belief):
`we are the only`, `we decide`, `that's not who we are`, `proven`,
`premium`, `we don't do`, `our customers expect`.

**Red-flag statements** — dig deeper when you hear these:

| Statement | Likely hidden belief |
|---|---|
| "I just want to help people" | Receiving / charging is selfish |
| "Money isn't the point for us" | Money is dirty / charging is wrong |
| "I'm not good at marketing" | Being visible is dangerous |
| "I hate selling" | Asking for money is wrong |
| "We can't find good customers" | Good customers aren't for us |
| "That's just how the market is" | Transparent belief about a ceiling |
| "We're not there yet" | We don't deserve success now |
| "If I don't check it, it goes wrong" | Identity = being indispensable |

## Result → belief mapping

Read these business results backwards into beliefs.

| Observed result | Candidate beliefs |
|---|---|
| **Underpricing / frequent discounting** | "We're not worth more", "people won't pay", "charging more is greedy" |
| **Revenue / growth plateau** | "This is as good as it gets", "growth means more problems", "we can't handle more" |
| **Low or sporadic visibility** | "Being seen is dangerous", "we'll be criticised", "the work should speak for itself" |
| **Message churn (new positioning every quarter)** | "Marketing doesn't work for us", outsourced judgement, learned helplessness |
| **Associates with everyone / won't pick a niche** | Fear of exclusion, fear of alienation, "we serve everyone" |
| **Copies competitors** | Outsourced judgement, "they must know something we don't" |
| **Won't stand next to larger brands** | Belonging / status belief — "we don't belong in that room" |
| **Owner overrides every hire** | Expert/Imposter identity — "nobody does it right but me" |
| **Takes wrong-fit clients "just in case"** | Scarcity — "if we say no it won't come again" |

## The five identity patterns

Identity patterns are **polarised** — the person asserts one pole and resists
its opposite. Name a pattern only when the evidence supports it, and always as a
hypothesis. State the asserted pole, the resisted pole, and the core fear.

### 1. Helper / Martyr
- **Asserted:** "I help others selflessly."
- **Resisted:** "I receive abundantly for my work."
- **Core fear:** "If I receive, I'm selfish / bad."
- **Shows up as:** undercharging, over-delivering, can't accept praise or payment, exhaustion, free offers dominate.

### 2. Expert / Imposter
- **Asserted:** "I know what I'm doing."
- **Resisted:** "I'm a fraud who will be found out."
- **Core fear:** "I'll be exposed as not good enough."
- **Shows up as:** over-qualifying, can't delegate ("nobody does it right"), constant comparison, hedging language.

### 3. Visible / Invisible
- **Asserted:** "I want to be seen and successful."
- **Resisted:** "Being seen is dangerous."
- **Core fear:** "If truly seen, I'll be attacked / rejected."
- **Shows up as:** inconsistent marketing, self-sabotage before launches, avoids video/speaking, retreat after visibility.

### 4. Abundant / Scarce
- **Asserted:** "I believe in abundance."
- **Resisted:** "There isn't enough."
- **Core fear:** "If I don't grab it now, it won't come again."
- **Shows up as:** fear-based decisions, wrong-fit clients, discounting to secure a sale, overworking, can't say no.

### 5. Worthy / Unworthy
- **Asserted:** "I deserve success."
- **Resisted:** "I'm not good enough."
- **Core fear:** "At my core, I'm fundamentally flawed."
- **Shows up as:** upper-limit problems, self-sabotage at success points, can't celebrate wins, constant striving.

## The creation loop

When a belief is identified as growth-blocking, show *how it sustains itself* —
a short loop the user can recognise:

```
BELIEF → ACTION → RESULT → INTERPRETATION ("see, it's true") → REINFORCED BELIEF
```

Example:
```
"We're not worth premium prices"
  → underprice
  → attract price-sensitive, difficult clients
  → get pushback on every quote
  → "see, our market won't pay more"
  → belief hardens
```

The loop is the most useful single artefact for the user — it turns "you have a
belief" into "here is the machine, and here is where to break it."

## Root fear

Under the surface beliefs there is usually one deepest fear. It is almost always
a variant of:

- "I'm not enough."
- "I'll be rejected / abandoned."
- "I'll be attacked / destroyed."
- "I'll lose something important."
- "I'll be exposed / shamed."

Name it only when several surface beliefs converge on it, and hold it gently —
this is the most sensitive line in the whole profile.

## Psychometric integration

If the harvest contains Kolbe A, Wealth Dynamics, DISC, or similar data, fold it
into the **Personality signals** line of the person's profile and use it to
*corroborate* — never to override — the evidence-based belief findings. A
psychometric label is a hypothesis generator, not a conclusion.

## Discovery questions (for thin harvests)

If the material is too thin to profile a dimension, these are the questions to
suggest the user asks the decision-maker — never answer them yourself:

- *What would happen if you doubled your prices?*
- *What stops you from being more visible?*
- *What would you do differently if you knew it would work?*
- *What would you have to give up to succeed at the next level?*
- *What do you believe is "just how this market works"?*
